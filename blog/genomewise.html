GenomeWise: RAG-Based Genomic
Variant Annotation

March 2026  ·  12 min read  ·  Shaza Azher
DNABERT FAISS RAG Python Local LLM Bioinformatics

Genomic variant annotation — the process of attaching biological meaning to mutations detected in sequencing data — remains one of the most labor-intensive bottlenecks in clinical genomics. GenomeWise is a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline designed to automate and improve this process, combining DNABERT embeddings, FAISS vector search, and a locally-hosted large language model.

The Problem

When a sequencing lab identifies a variant of uncertain significance (VUS), clinicians currently rely on a patchwork of databases — ClinVar, OMIM, gnomAD — and manual literature review. This process is slow, inconsistent, and difficult to scale. For South Asian populations in particular, sparse representation in reference databases compounds the uncertainty.

Variant interpretation is not a lookup problem. It is a reasoning problem. RAG lets us treat it as one.

Architecture

GenomeWise operates in three stages. First, variant sequences are embedded using DNABERT — a transformer pretrained on the human genome — producing dense vector representations that encode functional and structural context. These embeddings are indexed in a FAISS vector store alongside curated annotations from public databases.

At inference time, a query variant is embedded and retrieved against the FAISS index. The top-k most similar annotated variants are fetched as context. This context, combined with a structured prompt describing the query variant, is passed to a locally-hosted LLM (Ollama, quantized Mistral or LLaMA 3) for final interpretation generation.

The entire pipeline runs locally — no patient data leaves the clinical environment, which is a hard requirement for production genomics work.

Key Results

In preliminary evaluations on a held-out set of previously classified ClinVar variants, GenomeWise achieved 81% concordance with expert classifications at tier-1 pathogenicity calls. Retrieval precision at k=5 averaged 0.74 across variant classes. Performance was notably lower on variants with limited database representation — underscoring the data equity problem the system was partly designed to address.

What's Next

The immediate roadmap includes expanding the knowledge base to incorporate South Asian-specific genomic studies, fine-tuning DNABERT on variant-rich regions, and building an evaluation harness for clinical validation. A preprint is in preparation for submission to bioRxiv, with a target journal of Bioinformatics or Briefings in Bioinformatics.

GenomeWise is the technical core of a broader research agenda: making clinical genomics work for populations that have historically been excluded from the datasets that power it.
