How Transformers Actually Work
(5-Part Series)

Ongoing · TikTok / Instagram · Shaza Azher

Transformers are the architecture behind GPT, BERT, DALL·E, Whisper, and virtually every frontier AI system you've heard of. But most explanations of how they work are either too shallow ("it's like attention, basically") or too dense (here's the full Vaswani et al. paper, good luck). This series lives in between.

GRWM while I explain multi-head self-attention. No, really.

The @sheezusolves format is deliberate: get-ready-with-me beauty content fused with deep technical explainers. The juxtaposition is the point. Technical knowledge doesn't require a particular aesthetic. And beauty content doesn't require intellectual surrender.

The Series

EP 01
The Attention Mechanism, Explained with Highlighters
What does "attention" actually mean? We start with the intuition: when you read a sentence, not all words matter equally. Transformers learn which ones do.
EP 02
Keys, Queries, and Values (It's Not That Scary)
The QKV framework broken down without a single matrix equation. What each vector represents, and why the dot product actually makes intuitive sense.
EP 03
Multi-Head Attention: Why One Isn't Enough
Running attention in parallel, across different "representation subspaces." Why this gives transformers richer understanding than single-head attention.
EP 04
Positional Encoding: How Transformers Know Word Order
Unlike RNNs, transformers process tokens in parallel — so they need a way to inject sequence information. Sinusoidal encoding, explained.
EP 05
Training Dynamics: Loss, Backprop, and Why Scale Works
How transformers actually learn. From cross-entropy loss to the empirical scaling laws that changed how the field thinks about model development.

Why This Format

The technical AI space has a communication problem. The people who understand transformers deeply rarely make content accessible to curious non-experts. The people who make accessible content often strip out the parts that actually matter. I'm trying to do neither.

You ask. Sheezu solves.
